0. HOW TO READ THIS DOCUMENT — A NOTE ON NUMBERS
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Every concrete number below is one of two things, and each is labelled:

  [GT-A]   A real figure from the practice (variant A) ground truth or
           the shipped example reports. Use these to build and rehearse.

  [COMPUTED] A figure our own analyzer computes at run time. In the
           product it is ALWAYS rendered from the report's derivation
           field — never typed by a human, never hardcoded.

NON-NEGOTIABLE PRODUCT RULE: the sealed run on Day 6 uses a DIFFERENT
dataset (different days, tenants, slices). Nothing in the UI may be
hardcoded to variant A. The screen renders whatever loop-report.json
contains. If a number is on the screen, it exists in the report with a
derivation string behind it. This single rule protects the 18
"traceable to data" points and the whole credibility of the demo.

======================================================================
1. PRODUCT THESIS (ONE SENTENCE, SHARPENED)
======================================================================

Nexus Loop turns eight weeks of an AI agent's own logs into a queue of
VERIFIED DECISIONS — each one saying what broke, who it hurt, why, what
it costs, the fix, proof the fix works, and the human's recorded
approval — and it refuses, out loud, to answer what the data cannot
support.

The promise to the operator:

    "Don't investigate. Decide."

The product is an OPERATIONS CONTROL CENTER for AI-agent reliability,
not a dashboard, not a chatbot, not an LLM that summarizes transcripts.

======================================================================
2. THE WINNING STRATEGY (SCORING-AWARE PRODUCT PLAN)
======================================================================

The rubric is 100 points: 55 machine (scored by score.py, deterministic)
+ 45 human (scored by judges reading the screen and watching the demo).

The 45 human points decompose as:

    18  Decision quality  — can an operator act on each finding? Is every
                            impact figure traceable to data? Is the
                            baseline explicitly named?
    15  The screen        — can a stranger read a finding, see the
                            evidence, approve/reject? Does every number
                            carry its definition?
    12  Framing           — demo opens on the problem from the operator's
                            side; lookalike + refusal shown; coverage
                            stated out loud; fakes admitted.

THE INSIGHT THAT DECIDES OUR SCOPE (straight from the spec):
  "A Level-1 system with an excellent screen and decision layer can
   score ~70/100. A Level-3 system with neither can score ~58/100.
   Depth on ONE problem beats breadth across all eight sections."

Therefore our product bet is:

  BET 1 — DEPTH, NOT BREADTH. One hero finding taken flawlessly through
          the entire loop, rendered beautifully, beats three shallow
          findings. We build 5 screens superbly, not 20 adequately.

  BET 2 — THE SCREEN IS THE PRODUCT. The analyzer earns the 55 machine
          points; the SCREEN + FRAMING earn the 45 that most teams
          leave on the table. We invest disproportionately here.

  BET 3 — THE REFUSAL IS THE CLOSER. The spec calls the refusal "the
          most memorable 30 seconds of the demo." We design the demo to
          end on it.

  BET 4 — HONESTY IS A FEATURE, NOT A DISCLAIMER. Coverage, fidelity,
          calibration, and "we did NOT flag this" are shown proudly on
          the screen. This is where precision-minded judges award trust.

3. THE OPERATOR (WHO WE BUILD FOR) AND THEIR JOBS
======================================================================

PRIMARY PERSONA — "Priya, AI-Agent Operations Owner"
  Owns the health of a deployed WhatsApp banking/retail agent. Today she
  finds out things broke from customer complaints, then spends hours in
  logs guessing. She is accountable but under-tooled. She does not write
  SQL at 2am.

  Her jobs-to-be-done:
    JTBD-1  "Tell me if something is actually wrong — not just noisy."
    JTBD-2  "Show me who's hurt and what it costs, in numbers I can quote
             to my boss."
    JTBD-3  "Tell me the likely cause and what changed."
    JTBD-4  "Give me a fix and prove it works before I ship it."
    JTBD-5  "Keep me in control — I approve, the system records it."
    JTBD-6  "Don't make something up when you don't know."

SECONDARY PERSONAS (who a finding gets ROUTED to — the "owner" field):
    agent_builder   — owns prompts and KB content
    platform_owner  — owns tools/APIs and the runtime
    business_owner  — owns product launches and commercial impact

The product's job is to compress JTBD-1..6 into ONE screen per finding.

======================================================================
4. PRODUCT PRINCIPLES (THE RULES, AS UI BEHAVIOUR)
======================================================================

P1  A FINDING IS A DECISION, NOT A NUMBER.
    Never show "resolution dropped to 31%." Show the impact, cause,
    owner, cost of inaction, fix, proof, and the decision — together.

P2  EVERY NUMBER CARRIES ITS PROVENANCE.
    Each figure shows a fidelity badge (MEASURED / DERIVED / JUDGED /
    UNMEASURABLE) and expands to its definition, numerator, denominator,
    coverage, baseline, and derivation. (Trust model, Section 12.)

P3  COVERAGE IS SPOKEN OUT LOUD.
    Any tool-derived metric states "covers 72% of Acme traffic; v2 legacy
    flow emits no tool calls and is excluded." Honesty is on the surface,
    not in a footnote.

P4  WE SHOW WHAT WE DID NOT FLAG.
    Lookalikes get their own cards with a plain-language "why this is NOT
    a regression." Precision is a visible product feature.

P5  WE REFUSE, AND WE SAY WHAT WOULD MAKE IT ANSWERABLE.
    Unanswerable questions get a Gap card naming the exact missing event
    and its fields. Refusal is a first-class outcome, not an error state.

P6  THE HUMAN DECIDES; THE SYSTEM RECORDS.
    Approve/Reject is always present, always requires the decision to be
    written back into the report. A well-reasoned rejection is as good as
    an approval.

P7  THE MODEL IS BACKSTAGE.
    No "AI brain" imagery. No "AI insights" label on a logged fact. The
    operator sees evidence and decisions, not a model.

P8  NOTHING ON SCREEN IS UNTRACEABLE.
    If it renders, it came from the report. If it came from the report,
    it has a derivation. No exceptions. (This is P1+P2 as an engineering
    contract for the frontend.)

5. INFORMATION ARCHITECTURE (4 AREAS, 5 CORE SCREENS)
======================================================================

Four conceptual areas (the operator's mental model), NOT the software
modules:

  A. OBSERVE     system status, the deployment standard, coverage
  B. INVESTIGATE findings, evidence, lookalikes, measurement gaps
  C. IMPROVE     prescriptions, replay/verification
  D. GOVERN      decisions, audit trail, self-assessment

We SHIP five screens. Everything else is deferred (Section 10).

  SCREEN 1  CONTROL CENTER (home) — status + Action Required queue
  SCREEN 2  FINDING DETAIL (the hero screen) — the one-screen decision
  SCREEN 3  LOOKALIKE / "WHY NOT A PROBLEM" — the precision showcase
  SCREEN 4  MEASUREMENT GAPS / REFUSAL — the closer
  SCREEN 5  DECISION HISTORY + SELF-ASSESSMENT — the governance record

Navigation is a single left rail with exactly these five, plus a small
"Deployment Standard" and "Coverage" surfaced inside Observe. Resist
adding more. Focus reads as confidence.

6. THE HERO NARRATIVE — WHICH FAULT LEADS THE DEMO
======================================================================

We have three real faults to choose a hero from. Product recommendation:

  HERO = F2, THE SILENT TOOL FAILURE.
  Why: it is the fault a naive system CANNOT see. Every error dashboard
  on earth reports this API as 100% healthy (HTTP 200, outcome 'ok'),
  yet customers are being failed. When we say "every error metric reads
  flat — and we caught it anyway," that is the mic-drop that separates us
  from teams who threshold on error rate. It is judgment made visible.

  SECOND FINDING = F1, THE KB GAP.
  Why: it showcases the "no before-period" baseline problem. A new
  product launched with nothing in the KB, so there is no history to
  compare to — we baseline against PEER intents. Naming the baseline
  explicitly is worth real Decision-Quality points.

  THIRD (if time) = F3, THE COST REGRESSION.
  Why: resolution is FLAT; only turns and cost move. It proves we detect
  problems that never touch the outcome metric — via the deployment's own
  standard, not a threshold.

  DEMO LOOKALIKE = D1, THE TRAFFIC-MIX SHIFT.
  Why: it is counterintuitive and visual. A marketing campaign 5x'd a
  trivially-easy intent, so AGGREGATE containment actually went UP while
  every real cohort was flat or worse (Simpson's paradox). "It looks like
  good news. It's neither. Here's why we stratified." (D2 flash-sale and
  D3 rubric-change are also dismissed, shown briefly.)

  REFUSAL = A11, FAILOVER RATE. The closer.

The three faults, with the REAL variant-A shape (build & rehearse
against these; the sealed set differs):

  F1  KB gap — Acme Bank / intent premium_card_info
      [GT-A] new product launches day 34; KB pack shipped same day has no
      matching content; kb_hit false, kb_top_score 0.11–0.38.
      [GT-A] cohort resolution ~0.31 vs peer ~0.76; no before-period.
      Owner: agent_builder + business_owner. Fix class: kb.add.

  F2  Silent tool failure — Northwind Retail / intent order_status /
      tool get_order_status
      [GT-A] onset day 40 ("order service migration" 3.2.0->3.3.0).
      [GT-A] ~14% of calls return HTTP 200 with an EMPTY body; outcome
      'ok'; declared tool_error_rate 0.0145 -> 0.0156 (FLAT).
      [GT-A] order_status resolution 0.852 -> 0.776; same-tool retry then
      unresolved end. Detected via response_bytes / result_field_count on
      a 200, plus retry_count. Owner: platform_owner. Fix: tool.validate
      / tool.fallback.

  F3  Prompt regression — Acme Bank / agent acme_main_v3
      [GT-A] onset day 46 (prompt p_v3->p_v4 "safety and confirmation
      wording"). Median turns 4 -> 6, cost/session +~80%, resolution
      0.789 -> 0.803 (FLAT). Owner: agent_builder. Fix: prompt.edit /
      revert.

SCREEN 2 — FINDING DETAIL (THE HERO SCREEN)
----------------------------------------------------------------------

This is where 18 (decision quality) + a large share of 15 (screen)
points are won. It follows a strict narrative order. Every section maps
to a required "finding must contain" element from the spec.

Worked example: F2 (numbers are the variant-A target; product renders
from the report).

  ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
  │ FINDING F2                          Severity: CRITICAL             │
  │ Order-status answers are silently failing                         │
  │ State: VERIFIED · AWAITING DECISION      [state chip, Section 13]  │
  └──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

  1) WHAT HAPPENED
     The order-status tool is returning success with an empty answer.
     The runtime logs every call as 'ok' (HTTP 200), so error dashboards
     look clean — but ~14% of calls come back with nothing to say, and
     the customer's question goes unresolved. [COMPUTED]

  2) WHO IS AFFECTED / IMPACT  (the Impact Card — Section 11)
     Conversations affected ........ [COMPUTED] over days 40–52
     Share of tenant traffic ....... [COMPUTED]% of Northwind in window
     Resolution (order_status) ..... 0.852 -> 0.776   [GT-A]
     Additional unresolved ......... [COMPUTED]  (vs the pre-day-40 rate)
     Unplanned handoffs ............ [COMPUTED]
     Days running .................. 12  [GT-A: onset day 40]
     Cost of inaction .............. plain sentence, see (8)
     Every figure has a "how computed" expander. [P8]

  3) AFFECTED SLICE
     Tenant: Northwind Retail · Intent: order_status ·
     Tool: get_order_status · Window: day 40–52

  4) EVIDENCE  (the Evidence Chain — clickable, Section 14)
     [Resolution down for order_status]
        -> [tool_error_rate FLAT — this is why it was invisible]
        -> [response_bytes = 0 / result_field_count = 0 on 200s spikes]
        -> [same-tool retry_count > 0, then unresolved end]
        -> [config day 40: get_order_status 3.2.0 -> 3.3.0,
            "order service migration"]

  5) DIAGNOSIS
     Cause class: tool.contract_break (confidence [COMPUTED]).
     The order-service migration on day 40 changed the tool's contract;
     it now returns 200 with an empty payload on a subset of calls. The
     agent has nothing to answer with, retries the same tool, then ends
     unresolved.

  6) WHAT CHANGED  (the Timeline — Section 15)
     Horizontal: day 40 config marker aligned to the behaviour change.

  7) PROPOSED FIX  (the Decision framing — Section 8 of the report)
     Treat an empty/again-empty tool response as a failed call and take
     the defined fallback path instead of answering blind.
     Change type: tool.validate (fallback: tool.fallback).
     Asking approval for: "Ship response-validation on get_order_status."
     Risk if diagnosis is wrong: "Adds a fallback branch that could fire
       on legitimately empty results (e.g. no orders found)."
     Would NOT ship if: "empty is a valid business answer for this
       intent and validation would suppress it."

  8) DID THE FIX WORK  (Replay before/after — Section 16)
     BEFORE  resolution 0.78     AFTER  [COMPUTED, from replay rp_...]
     Verdict: IMPROVED / NO EFFECT / REGRESSED  [from replay]
     Golden set: passed (no known-good conversations broke) [from replay]
     "A no-effect result is shown honestly, not hidden."

  9) COST OF INACTION (one plain sentence, grounded in the numbers)
     "If nobody acts, roughly [COMPUTED] more Northwind customers per week
      will ask about their order and be silently failed, and [COMPUTED]
      of them will be pushed to a human who wasn't meant to be involved."

  10) OWNER
     platform_owner — the tool contract changed; the platform team owns
     get_order_status. (Owner is derived from cause class — Section 9.)

  11) DECISION GATE
     [ REJECT ]                                     [ APPROVE FIX ]
     Reason (required): free text + quick reasons
       (Incorrect diagnosis / Insufficient evidence / Replay weak /
        Risk too high / Other)
     On submit: writes prescriptions[].approval {verdict, decided_by,
     reason, at} back into loop-report.json. [P6]

Design intent: a stranger can read top to bottom and act, without ever
opening a second screen. That is literally the Screen rubric.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
SCREEN 3 — LOOKALIKE / "WHY THIS IS NOT A PROBLEM"
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Purpose: earn the 15 Specificity machine points AND the Framing points,
by showing precision as a designed outcome. This is a signature screen.

Hero lookalike: D1 (traffic-mix shift).

  ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
  │ EXAMINED — NOT A REGRESSION            Acme Bank · days 22–31      │
  │ "Containment went UP. We still did not celebrate — or flag it."   │
  └──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

  WHAT LOOKED SUSPICIOUS
    Aggregate containment rose from 0.812 to 0.832. [GT-A] A naive
    detector would call this an improvement.

  WHY IT IS NOT A QUALITY EVENT (either direction)
    A campaign drove 5x volume into branch_locator — a trivially
    contained intent (share 13% -> 44%). [GT-A] The aggregate moved
    because the MIX moved, not because the agent got better. Stratified
    by intent, every real cohort is flat or slightly worse.

  EVIDENCE
    · branch_locator share: 0.133 -> 0.437  [GT-A]
    · per-intent resolution during window: branch_locator 0.90,
      balance_enquiry 0.85, txn_dispute 0.63, product_info 0.72 [GT-A]
    · no config marker explains a quality change here

  VERDICT: not_a_regression — cause class traffic_mix. Confidence: high.

Then two compact cards for the other two, so all three are on record:
  D2 (load_event, Northwind, days 12–15): flash sale 4x volume; p95
     latency doubled then self-corrected; quality flat throughout. Not a
     quality regression — a transient platform load event. [GT-A]
  D3 (judge_version_boundary, both tenants, day 28): quality rubric
     v1->v2 dropped judged scores ~0.6 across BOTH tenants at once. We do
     NOT trend judged quality across the boundary; judge_version is
     stamped on every value. Not a regression — a measurement change.
     [GT-A]

Product note: filing all three as is_regression:false WITH a reason AND
the matching cause class is exactly what score.py rewards. The screen and
the report say the same thing.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
SCREEN 4 — MEASUREMENT GAPS / THE REFUSAL (THE CLOSER)
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Purpose: the most memorable 30 seconds. We refuse an operator question
and specify precisely what would make it answerable.

  ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
  │ REFUSED — CANNOT BE MEASURED                                       │
  │ Operator asked (A11): "What is our failover rate — how often did a │
  │ primary model or tool fail and an alternate serve the user?"       │
  └──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

  OUR ANSWER: We will not give you a number.

  WHY  There is no failover mechanism in this runtime, so no failover
       event is ever emitted. There is no primary/secondary path to
       observe. A rate would be invented, not measured.

  THE TEMPTING WRONG ANSWER  llm_call.retry_count > 0 looks like a proxy.
       It is not — those are QUALITY retries at a different temperature.
       Reporting them as failovers would read as "0% failover," i.e. as
       good news, and mislead you into trusting resilience you don't have.

  WHAT WOULD MAKE THIS ANSWERABLE  add this event to the runtime:
       event name: failover        grain: step
       fields:  from_target, to_target, reason, recovered
       owner:   conversation-runtime

  This card writes gaps[] {ask_id A11, verdict NOT_MEASURABLE, why,
  nearest_proxy, why_the_proxy_misleads, required_event} to the report.

Also on this screen (smaller): the OTHER honest-refusal patterns, because
they earn points too:
  · A09 "how many gave up out of frustration?" — abandonment is MEASURED,
    but the REASON is JUDGED and needs a versioned, calibrated judge.
    Classifying it as measured would be a Rule-1 violation. We answer with
    a judged signal + its calibration, not a raw count.
  · customer_ref breakdown — REFUSED. It is near-unique (cardinality
    budget 200); breaking a metric down by it is a planner error. We cite
    the budget instead of melting the store.

SCREEN 5 — DECISION HISTORY + SELF-ASSESSMENT (GOVERNANCE)

Purpose: prove the loop closes and the system keeps its own record.

DECISION HISTORY (audit trail — every decision is an event)
  F2 · tool.validate · APPROVED · by <judge/operator> · <ts>

                golden set passed."
  F1 · kb.add · APPROVED · reason: "Clear KB gap, low-risk content add."
  (A rejection with a good reason is shown as an equally valid outcome.)

SELF-ASSESSMENT (the return arrow — HONEST, small, real)
  Cycles recorded: [COMPUTED, the real number of prescription
    prediction-vs-outcome pairs we actually ran]
  Per change-type: predicted delta vs replay-observed delta, hit rate.
  What the loop now trusts less (down-weighted change types), if any.

  HARD PRODUCT RULE: this panel shows the TRUE hit rate over the fixes we
  actually verified. We do NOT display "91% over 22 findings." There are
  three faults. Inflating this is exactly the kind of hidden fake the
  spec calls disqualifying. Small and honest beats big and fake.

======================================================================
8. THE DECISION OBJECT (WHAT "APPROVE" ACTUALLY MEANS)
======================================================================

Every prescription is framed as a decision the human can own. On screen
and in the report, a fix always carries:

  asking_approval_for   — in the operator's words
  predicted_delta       — metric, from, to (from the analyzer, not the LLM)
  risk_if_diagnosis_wrong
  would_not_ship_if     — the condition that should STOP the change
  autonomy_rung         — L0..L4 (we recommend L1/L2: system proposes,
                          human approves; never auto-ship)

On decision, we persist:
  verdict (accepted/rejected/deferred), decided_by, reason, at.

Product stance on autonomy: Nexus Loop NEVER ships to the live agent. It
proposes and proves; the human approves; we record. "Autonomous
analysis + human governance" is the pitch. Auto-shipping would violate
the spec's human-in-the-loop requirement and read as reckless to judges.

======================================================================
9. SEVERITY & OWNER — DETERMINISTIC, DEFENSIBLE, ON-SCREEN
======================================================================

Judges reward traceability. So severity and owner are NOT vibes — they
are computed and explained.

SEVERITY (computed; the formula is shown in a tooltip):
  Inputs: user_harm (resolution/quality deficit vs baseline, OR
          standard-relative deviation for flat-outcome faults),
          blast_radius (share of tenant traffic in window),
          duration (days running), ongoing? (self-corrected or not).

  Bands:
    CRITICAL — customer-facing outcome harmed AND (large share OR large
               deficit) AND still ongoing. (F2: customers silently failed,
               ongoing, high-volume intent.)
    HIGH     — clear regression but narrower blast radius, OR a
               cost/efficiency regression with flat outcomes.
               (F1: high; F3: high — resolution flat, cost is the harm.)
    MEDIUM   — real but small deviation, limited exposure.
    LOW      — borderline; logged for transparency.

  The tooltip literally states the inputs used for this finding. That is
  how "is the baseline named / is it traceable" gets its points.

OWNER (derived from cause class — a fixed mapping, shown as a reason):
    kb.gap            -> agent_builder (+ business_owner if launch-linked)
    tool.contract_break / tool.outage -> platform_owner
    prompt.regression -> agent_builder
    model.change      -> agent_builder (+ platform_owner)
    routing.error     -> agent_builder
    traffic_mix / load / judge_change -> no owner (not a regression)

10. WHAT WE DELIBERATELY CUT (PM DISCIPLINE)
======================================================================

Cutting is the job. Each of these was in the earlier Blueprint and is
CUT or DEFERRED, with the reason:

  CUT — "Ask Nexus" free-text chatbot. It invites a Rule-1 violation
        (asking the model for a logged fact) and reads as the exact
        "LLM wrapper" the spec warns against. HIGH downside, low upside.
        If we want a Q&A affordance, it is a DETERMINISTIC router over
        precomputed metrics with hardcoded refusals — not a chatbot.

  CUT — Notifications / attention-management system. The Action Required
        queue already prioritizes. Extra plumbing, no points.

  CUT — Separate "Fix Studio" module. Folded into the Finding Detail
        (section 7 of that screen). One place to decide.

  DEFER — System Health panel. Nice for trust, but the demo's trust comes
        from evidence, not a green "workers: healthy" light. Build only
        if Screens 1–5 are done.

  DEFER — "Demo Mode / Story Mode." We rehearse the real product instead
        of building a scripted overlay. A scripted demo that hides the
        real flow is a framing risk if a judge asks to click freely.

  DEFER — Elaborate settings, profiles, admin, multi-chart analytics
        pages, decorative animations. FM11: a beautiful UI with no
        evidence is a failure mode.

Rule of thumb: if a feature does not help win Decision Quality, the
Screen, Framing, or a machine point, it does not ship this week.

======================================================================
11. THE IMPACT CARD (SPEC — REUSED ON EVERY FINDING)
======================================================================

The single most important visual element. It answers, for every
regression, the exact questions the spec's "finding must contain" list
demands. Every field renders from impact{} in the report:

  conversations_affected      integer   [COMPUTED]
  share_of_traffic            % of tenant traffic in the window
  downstream:
     would_have_resolved_at_baseline  (how many would have resolved)
     unplanned_handoffs               (reached a human unnecessarily)
     abandoned                        (gave up)
     extra_turns / repeat_contacts    (where relevant, e.g. F3)
  cost_usd                    number OR null ("null is an honest answer")
  days_running                integer
  derivation                  the sentence that lets a reader re-check
                              every number above, INCLUDING the baseline
                              and WHY that baseline was chosen.

Worked derivation, F1 [GT-A, from the shipped example — this is the bar]:
  "239 sessions matched the cohort over 11 days (4.2% of this tenant's
   traffic in the window). Observed resolution 0.314 against a baseline
   of 0.816 — this cohort has NO before-period, so the baseline is other
   question-answering intents on the same agent in the same window — so
   120 conversations that would have resolved did not. 149 ended in a
   handoff the agent was not built to make; 15 were abandoned. cost_usd
   is summed over the cohort and covers v3 traffic only."

That paragraph IS the product. It names the baseline, justifies it,
counts the human harm, and states coverage. Every finding gets one.

======================================================================
12. THE TRUST MODEL (FIDELITY BADGES) — A SIGNATURE FEATURE
======================================================================

Every number wears a badge, and every badge expands. This is how we make
honest measurement visible.

  MEASURED     directly from logs / deterministic calc. (green)
  DERIVED      computed from measured values (e.g. silent_tool_success
               from response_bytes on a 200). (blue)
  JUDGED       needs a model to read meaning; shows judge_version AND
               calibration (agreement with human labels). (amber)
  UNMEASURABLE required data does not exist; links to the Gap. (grey)

CALIBRATION RULE ON SCREEN: a judged metric shows its agreement with the
human label set (labels/: 400 outcome + 96 rubric). We never show 1.00 —
human labellers disagree ~7.5% of the time, so a perfect judge is a bug,
not a triumph. The screen says so in the tooltip. (score.py penalizes a
reported 1.00; our product turns that constraint into a trust cue.)

COVERAGE ON SCREEN: tool-derived metrics display, e.g., "coverage 0.72
(Acme) / 0.93 (Northwind) — v2 legacy flow emits no tool calls and is
excluded from the denominator." We never blend the two tenants into one
misleading figure.

======================================================================
13. THE FINDING STATE MODEL (ONE LIFECYCLE, SHOWN EVERYWHERE)
======================================================================

Every finding shows where it is in the loop. Same chip on the home
queue, the detail header, and the audit trail:

  DETECTED -> VALIDATED -> DIAGNOSED -> FIX PROPOSED -> REPLAYED ->
  VERIFIED -> DECISION PENDING -> APPROVED / REJECTED -> RECORDED

Lookalikes short-circuit at VALIDATED -> DISMISSED (not_a_regression).
Gaps live outside this flow entirely (REFUSED).

This makes "how much of detect->diagnose->prescribe->verify runs linked
and unattended" (the 8 loop points) legible to a human at a glance.

======================================================================
14. THE EVIDENCE CHAIN (AUDITABLE REASONING, NOT AN LLM ANSWER)
======================================================================

The diagnosis is presented as a chain of clickable facts, each linking
to the underlying data view. It converts "the AI thinks X" into "here is
the trail." For F2:

  metric change -> invisibility proof -> discriminating field ->
  behaviour -> config marker

  [order_status resolution down]
      -> [tool_error_rate flat: why the obvious metric missed it]
      -> [response_bytes=0 / result_field_count=0 on 200s]
      -> [same-tool retry then unresolved end]
      -> [config day 40: get_order_status 3.2.0->3.3.0]

Each node opens the relevant slice (sessions, steps, the config row).
This is a top differentiator: the reasoning is inspectable, so a
skeptical judge can verify it instead of trusting it.

======================================================================
15. THE TIMELINE ("WHAT CHANGED?")
======================================================================

A horizontal incident timeline per finding, aligning config markers to
behaviour changes. It is the visual argument for attribution.

  ... day 34 KB pack ...  day 40 [order svc migration] === behaviour
  change here === ... day 46 prompt change ...

Guardrail against misattribution (a real trap in the data): there are
NEARBY changes that are NOT the cause. For F3 (day 46 prompt) there is a
day-41 routing change on the same agent; the timeline + evidence must
show the behaviour change tracks the PROMPT marker, not routing. The
product shows candidate markers and why the losing ones were rejected —
that honesty is itself decision-quality signal.

======================================================================
16. REPLAY / VERIFICATION LAB (BEFORE / AFTER, HONESTLY)
======================================================================

Presented inside the finding as a before/after strip. Rules that shape
the UX (from the replay contract):

  · We call replay ONCE per diagnosed fix. The budget is 40 total for the
    team; the endpoint is designed to punish brute-forcing. The UI shows
    "replay budget used: N / 40" so the operator (and judges) see we are
    disciplined, not probing.
  · A fix only moves the metric if it addresses the real cause. So a
    NO-EFFECT result is a legitimate, displayed outcome — never hidden,
    never retried in a loop.
  · We show the golden-set guard: "known-good conversations checked: X,
    regressed: 0" — a fix that helps the cohort but breaks good work is
    caught and shown.
  · prediction vs observed: we show predicted delta next to the replayed
    delta. Being honestly wrong about the size feeds Self-Assessment.

======================================================================
17. VISUAL LANGUAGE
======================================================================

  · "Mission control," not "AI marketing site." Dark, dense, calm.
  · Large impact numbers; clear severity badges; fidelity badges on
    every figure; timeline; evidence cards; before/after strips; strong
    primary action buttons (Approve/Reject).
  · No AI-brain imagery. No decorative motion. No stock gradients.
  · Accessibility: legible in both light and dark; color is never the
    only signal (badges carry text).
  · One accent color for "needs decision"; red reserved for CRITICAL and
    for REFUSED, so the two most important states pop.

======================================================================
18. THE DEMO RUNBOOK (10 MIN PRESENT + 5 MIN Q&A)
======================================================================

The spec fixes the ORDER. We follow it exactly. Open on the operator's
problem, never on architecture.

  0:00  COLD OPEN ON THE PROBLEM (Screen 1)
        "This is Priya's Monday. Her agent looks healthy on every error
         dashboard. Nexus Loop disagrees." Point at the CRITICAL card.

  1:00  THE FINDING + IMPACT (Screen 2, F2, sections 1–2)
        "Order-status answers are silently failing. Here's who's hurt and
         what it costs — every number expands to its derivation."

  2:30  THE LOOKALIKE WE IGNORED (Screen 3, D1)
        "Before you trust us on what IS wrong, watch what we refused to
         flag. Containment went UP. It's a mix shift, not an improvement.
         A naive detector gets this backwards." (Precision = trust.)

  4:00  THE DIAGNOSIS + CONFIG CHANGE (Screen 2, sections 4–6)
        Walk the evidence chain; land on the day-40 migration marker.
        "tool_error_rate is flat — that's WHY everyone else misses this."

  5:30  THE FIX (Screen 2, section 7)
        Show it framed as a decision: what we're asking, the risk, the
        ship-blocker.

  6:30  THE PROOF (Screen 2, section 8 — replay)
        Before/after on the affected cohort; golden set passed; budget
        used 1/40. "We proved it before asking you to ship it."

  7:30  THE HUMAN DECISION (Screen 2, section 11)
        A judge clicks APPROVE. Show it written into the report live.
        "The system proposes and proves. The human decides. We record."

  8:30  THE REFUSAL — THE CLOSER (Screen 4, A11)   *** memorable 30s ***
        "Last thing. The operator asked for our failover rate. Watch."
        Show REFUSED + the exact missing event spec.
        "We could have shown 0%. That would have been a lie that sounds
         like good news. So we told you what to instrument instead."

  9:00  THE RECORD (Screen 5, brief)
        Decision history + the honest self-assessment ("we verified N
        fixes; here's our real hit rate").

  9:30  ONE ARCHITECTURE SLIDE + THE 100x LINE
        "Today the corpus is ~30MB, so Nexus Loop runs as one
         deterministic pass — which is why it ran UNATTENDED on your
         sealed dataset with nothing to hand-hold. At 100x we shard by
         tenant/day onto a worker fleet; here's how." (This is where the
         engineering architecture from Approach.txt earns its keep — as
         the scaling story, not the build.)

  Q&A prep: know the coverage numbers, the baseline choice for F1, why
  D1/D2/D3 are not regressions, and the exact failover event fields.

19. THE ONE-PAGE NOTE (DRAFT — "REAL / STUBBED / SIMULATED / 100x")
======================================================================

This is scored under Framing (12). Honesty here is worth more than
polish. Draft, to live in the report's system_notes and as a slide:

  WHAT IS REAL
   · Detection, cohorting, baselines, coverage, calibration, cause
     classification, config attribution, impact counts, and gap specs are
     computed deterministically from the corpus. No LLM produces any
     scored number.
   · The loop-report.json is generated end-to-end from the sealed run
     with no manual edits.
   · Human approve/reject is recorded live and written back to the report.

  WHAT IS JUDGED (and calibrated)
   · Quality/satisfaction/abandonment-reason use a versioned LLM judge;
     we publish its agreement with the human label set (never 1.00).

  WHAT IS STUBBED / SIMULATED
   · The replay service is the provided local endpoint, not a live agent
     runtime; we treat its before/after as the verification signal.
   · [List any narrative text that is LLM-generated over computed
     evidence — it is presentation, not fact.]

  WHAT BREAKS AT 100x
   · Single-process in-memory analysis is fine at 30MB; at millions of
     conversations it becomes I/O bound. The fix is partitioning by
     tenant/day, a columnar analytical store, and a horizontally-scaled
     worker fleet on a queue — the architecture in Approach.txt. The
     product surface and the report contract are unchanged.
   · Replay budget (40) does not scale to many candidate fixes; at scale
     it becomes a rate-limited internal service with caching by
     (cohort, change) so we never re-probe the same hypothesis.

  We never: change a metric definition, change a judge version, or change
  the reviewed good set to make results look better.
